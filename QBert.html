Quality Control Program Guide — FY 2027

The Disaster Servicing and Liquidation Center
U.S. Small Business Administration
Office of Financial Program Operations
Updated: October 2026

I. Executive Summary

The Quality Control Program (the Program) is a process designed to help management effectively manage risk while achieving objectives in the post-servicing and liquidation actions of disaster home and business loans. These actions include assumptions, offers in compromise, charge-offs, transfers to liquidation, deferments, subordinations and substitutions of collateral, releases of collateral, insurance check recoveries, protective bidding, and related servicing and liquidation activities.

The Program integrates risk management with existing management processes, identifies future events that can have both positive and negative effects, and evaluates effective strategies for managing the Center’s exposure to possible future events. The Quality Program aligns strategy, people, processes, technology, and knowledge with a strategic emphasis on eliminating system inconsistency and supporting greater confidence in the integrity of SBA’s financial programs. High-level management attention is devoted to the quality initiative and related ongoing training.

One of the benefits of centralized operations is the ability to augment the skills and attention to detail already benefiting the Agency with an additional robust quality control program. The Program, outlined within this document, is the method used by the Center to verify and document compliance with Regulatory, Statutory, and policy requirements. It builds upon longstanding quality-related activities and coordinates key quality functions so they are reported in a transparent way.

The Program consists of three complementary components that together provide comprehensive coverage: Loan Reviews, Customer Service, and Aging. Loan reviews are conducted monthly using a risk-weighted sample methodology. Customer Service quality is assessed through both verbal interactions captured in the Genesys system and written interactions managed in the Customer Service Hub. Aging reviews monitor the timeliness of actions and case resolution across Teams.

II. Program Operations – Governance and Administration

The Disaster Servicing and Liquidation Center (the Center) of the Office of Financial Program Operations handles home and business disaster loans. The objective of the Center is to bring problem loans to resolution. The Center employs highly skilled finance, legal, and support professionals and uses lean systems designed to improve quality, eliminate waste, and reduce delays and total costs.

Staff are located in three offices: El Paso, Santa Ana, and Birmingham. Santa Ana functions solely as an office location where Center personnel work; it is not a separate Center. There is one Center, led by one Center Director and three Deputies.

Organizational Structure

Operations are organized into six Teams:

  • Resolution Group A
  • Resolution Group B
  • Administrative/PIF
  • Liquidation
  • TOP
  • Realty

Each Team is composed of Supervisory Loan Specialists, Lead Loan Specialists, Loan Specialists, and Loan Specialist Assistants. Team Supervisors are responsible for the quality of work performed by their staff and for implementing corrective actions identified through the Quality Control Program.

Quality Control Staffing

The Quality Control function is performed by two Quality Control Specialists:

  • Robert Thompson, Quality Control Specialist
  • Tonya Hayes, Quality Control Specialist

The Quality Control Specialists conduct the monthly reviews across all three Program components, document findings, recommend corrective actions, contribute to process improvements, and provide training recommendations. In instances of limited staffing, the number of reviews may be adjusted for a given month, with explanatory comments included on the monthly reports. Should a position be vacant for any period, coverage arrangements are made to avoid a complete lapse in reviews; full capacity resumes once the position is filled.

Quality Control Communication Plan

Quality is strived for throughout the loan servicing and liquidation process and is the focus of the entire Center staff. Recommendations for improvements are made to the Center Director based on observations of systems, processes, and best practices identified during reviews. Findings are reported formally. Generally, Center management is responsible for corrective actions. Findings and corrective actions are documented to support summary reporting to management.

Monthly reports are generated and distributed to the Center Director, Deputies, and Team Supervisors. These include metrics, a memo summarizing reviews, status of outstanding corrective actions, and an actions report as needed. Quality is incorporated into performance elements for Loan Specialists. Corrections are handled by Supervisors and tracked as re-work. Root-cause corrective actions are managed separately by Quality Control to ensure systemic issues are addressed accurately. Disputes on findings are resolved by the Center Director; if needed, issues may be elevated further.

III. The Three-Part Quality Control Program

The Quality Control Program operates through three integrated components:

1. Loan Reviews

Loan action reviews are performed on a monthly basis for now. Actions decisioned in the prior month are selected for review using a risk-weighted sampling methodology. The sample is not a flat percentage of all actions processed. Each loan action is assigned a risk rank (1 = highest risk). The pull rate for review rises with the risk rank, so a higher-risk action is reviewed at a higher percentage than a lower-risk action.

The percentage of high-risk actions selected for review is targeted to fall between 10% and 20% of all high-risk loan actions processed in the month. Lower-risk actions are reviewed at correspondingly lower rates based on their risk weight. In practice, a monthly sample of approximately 80 actions is drawn and split between the two Quality Control Specialists (as illustrated by the September 2026 review, which achieved a higher-risk review rate of approximately 15.6%).

Actions are pulled from the L-Track and SL-Track systems. Reviews assess compliance with Agency policy, procedures, documentation standards, and quality expectations. Findings are documented in the Quality Control Tracker and reported to the responsible Supervisory Loan Specialist.

2. Customer Service

The Customer Service component evaluates the quality of interactions with borrowers and other stakeholders. Reviews cover both verbal and written communications to ensure accuracy, professionalism, policy compliance, and a positive customer experience.

  • Verbal Customer Service – Assessed through the Genesys system. Quality Control reviews call recordings, interaction summaries, and related data for accuracy of information provided, professionalism, adherence to policy, and overall customer experience.
  • Written Customer Service – Assessed through the Customer Service Hub. Quality Control reviews written correspondence, case notes, responses, and documentation for completeness, accuracy, appropriate tone, and adherence to policy and procedures.

Findings from Customer Service reviews are documented in the Quality Control Tracker. Trends are analyzed monthly and used to identify training needs, job aids, and process improvements. Results are reported alongside loan review findings.

3. Aging

The Aging component monitors the timeliness of loan actions and case resolution across the Center. Quality Control tracks the age of open actions and cases by Team and action type to identify bottlenecks, delayed items, and opportunities to improve processing times and reduce overall cycle time.

Aging data is drawn from L-Track and SL-Track and is reviewed in conjunction with the monthly loan action reviews. Persistent aging issues are elevated through the corrective action process and may result in targeted process recommendations, resource adjustments, or training. The goal is to support efficient resolution of loans while maintaining quality.

IV. Quality Control Process Framework

Quality Control responsibilities include developing and implementing cost-effective programs to evaluate and strengthen internal controls; establishing guidelines and providing training related to internal controls; assisting program managers in identifying significant weaknesses and promoting timely and cost-effective corrective action; and establishing guidelines for internal quality reviews to effectively assess the condition of significant servicing and liquidation activities.

Finding Identification

Through the review process (Loan Reviews, Customer Service, and Aging), Quality Control identifies potential findings that, if they occur, will affect the Center, and determines whether they represent opportunities or might adversely affect the Center’s ability to successfully implement the Agency’s strategy. A finding is an incident or occurrence emanating from internal or external sources that affects implementation of strategy or achievement of objectives. Findings represent opportunities which management channels back into strategy and objective-setting processes.

When identifying findings, Quality Control considers a variety of internal (policy, procedural, or documentation errors) and external factors that may give rise to risk and opportunities, in the context of the full scope of the Center’s routine operations.

Root Cause Analysis

The basic reason for investigating and reporting the cause of occurrences is to enable the identification of corrective actions adequate to prevent recurrence. The investigation process is used to gain an understanding of the occurrences, their causes, and what corrective actions are necessary to prevent recurrence.

The line of reasoning in the investigation is:

  1. Identify the problem (condition, situation, or action that was not wanted and not planned).
  2. Determine what program element should have prevented this occurrence (was it lacking or did it fail).
  3. Investigate the reason why this situation was permitted to exist.

Root Cause Assessment includes the following:

  • Analysis of the events and causal factor chain
  • Determination of the significance of the problem (Critical or Non-Critical occurrence)
  • Determination of the dollar impact of the error, if applicable
  • Identification of the causes (Internal Process or External) immediately preceding and surrounding the problem (the reason the problem occurred)

Providing Recommendations

Accompanied with findings, Quality Control provides recommendations in an attempt to influence the Center’s strategies and objectives; how business line activities are structured; and how risks are identified, assessed, and acted upon. Recommendations tend to address the design and functioning of control activities, information and communication systems, and monitoring activities. Center Management has latitude and flexibility in implementing recommendations; however, management must document what action was taken to resolve findings.

Recommendations that accompany findings include:

  • How to correct the identified finding
  • How to improve the operations that potentially caused the finding
  • How management could provide greater oversight of the loan action approval process
  • How to improve management’s assignment of authority and responsibility
  • How to improve staff development and training opportunities
  • How to improve documentation of Center processes for identifying, assessing, and responding to internal risks

Corrective Action Process

Once Quality Control identifies a finding, root cause, and recommendation, Center Management determines how it will respond. In considering its response, management assesses the effect on risk likelihood and impact, as well as costs and benefits, selecting a response that brings residual risk within desired risk tolerances. Quality Control provides all relevant loan-level deficiency information to Center Management.

Before the corrective action is initiated, Quality Control provides Center Management all relevant information, which is captured and stored in the Quality Control Tracker. The Tracker maintains the loan-level deficiency details, allowing Center Management to review and close out the corrective action. Communication takes place by managing time expectations of curing the deficiency, responsible individuals and Teams, and other important matters. Communication of the corrective action is also carried downstream from Center Management to the responsible Loan Specialists and Team members to acknowledge errors and the events that caused the deficiency.

To close out a finding, Center Management provides possible risk mitigation strategies, including accepting a perceived low level of risk, developing additional controls, or instituting a process of independent testing to provide greater assurance that risks are mitigated to the extent necessary.

Effective corrective actions by Center Management include management emphasis on the identification and correction of problems that can affect staff performance, including assigning qualified staff to implement improvements. Supervisors typically provide an initial written response within 10 business days of receipt. Upon concurrence, agreed corrective action is taken within 30 days, after which the Quality Control Specialist closes the item in the Tracker. In the event of non-concurrence, the matter is supported with information for senior-level management to resolve at a higher level, typically through the monthly quality meeting or a follow-up discussion with the Center Director.

V. Tracking Systems

The Center uses an HTML-based Quality Control Tracker that links directly to both the L-Track and the SL-Track. These are the loan trackers used by all staff for loan action flow and actions. There is no longer a SharePoint-based system. Earlier references to Q-Bert have been superseded by the Quality Control Tracker.

Quality Control Tracker

The Quality Control Tracker is the central system for documenting and managing the Quality Control Program. It supports:

  • Documenting review findings from Loan Reviews, Customer Service (Genesys and Customer Service Hub), and Aging
  • Tracking corrective actions from identification through closure
  • Generating metrics, trend reports, and monthly summaries
  • Linking review results to the underlying loan actions and case data in L-Track and SL-Track

L-Track and SL-Track remain the primary systems used by Loan Specialists, Lead Loan Specialists, and Supervisory Loan Specialists for day-to-day action processing, status tracking, and workflow management. The Quality Control Tracker integrates with these systems so that review activity is connected to the operational record.

VI. Reporting and Metrics

Monthly reports are generated from the Quality Control Tracker after the Quality Control Specialists have completed the prior month’s reviews and entered results. Reports are distributed to the Center Director, Deputies, and Team Supervisors. Typical report contents include:

  • Summary of actions and interactions reviewed and findings identified
  • Corrective action status, aging of open items, and closures
  • Accuracy and quality metrics (the Center’s goal is high accuracy on reviewed items, historically targeted near 99% where applicable)
  • Trends by Team, action type, risk category, and Customer Service channel
  • Aging statistics for open actions and cases
  • Training recommendations and process improvement opportunities

All organizations, including government agencies such as SBA, are expected to demonstrate how their unique processes are performing and delivering measurable value. Metrics are a valuable tool because activities that are measured get done, and one cannot improve what one cannot measure. Internal metrics inform and train management and staff and help calibrate the delivery of services. External metrics are relied upon by investigators and auditors and represent areas of greater potential risk.

The Quality Control Specialists prepare a narrative summary discussing reviews by type, trends, recommended training, and any potential issues identified. Special or targeted reviews completed during the month are also discussed.

VII. Training and Policy Recommendations

Providing training recommendations to Center management is an essential function of the Quality Control Program. Recommendations are provided on a monthly basis and are determined based on deficiency trends identified through the review process across Loan Reviews, Customer Service, and Aging. Training is generally conducted on a monthly basis; in instances where training cannot be held in a particular month, additional training is provided in the following months.

Training addressing the specified areas of concern is presented in a manner deemed appropriate by the trainer (for example, case study, lecture, or hands-on review) and can be provided in a group or individual setting. Quality Control supports the Center’s quality initiative by helping identify and deliver training that addresses root causes.

The results of the reviews are also used to recommend Agency policy and internal process enhancements. Error trends related to frequently misinterpreted or obsolete SOP requirements provide an opportunity for the Agency to enhance its existing policy. Depending on the frequency and risk impact associated with the errors, a recommendation to revise the SOP, issue a Policy Notice, or enhance existing or develop new job aids (program guides and checklists) may be presented to management.

When making a policy recommendation, a memorandum is drafted by the Quality Control Specialist discussing the errors identified and the subsequent impact to the Agency in support of the proposed recommendation. Center management reviews the recommendation and advises the Quality Control Specialist of its decision to support or decline. Management discusses any decisions to decline or amend the recommendation with the Quality Control Specialist; should the Specialist not agree with the Center’s determination, the recommendation can be elevated for further consideration.

VIII. Quality Control Special Projects

The Quality Control Specialists work closely with Headquarters on projects as assigned, including the development and implementation of quality improvement initiatives. Projects may be assigned to address certain issues identified by the Office of Inspector General or other audit groups. As projects are assigned, they are detailed and tracked in coordination with Center management.

IX. Appendix

Monthly Review Process Summary

  1. Pull the prior month’s completed actions from L-Track and SL-Track.
  2. Apply the risk-weighted sampling methodology to select the Loan Review sample, targeting 10–20% of high-risk actions.
  3. Conduct Customer Service reviews using Genesys (verbal interactions) and the Customer Service Hub (written interactions).
  4. Review Aging data for open actions and cases by Team and action type.
  5. Assign the review work between the two Quality Control Specialists.
  6. Perform the reviews and document findings in the Quality Control Tracker.
  7. Notify Supervisory Loan Specialists of findings requiring corrective action.
  8. Track responses and closures; prepare monthly reports and metrics covering all three Program components.

Definitions

  • Quality Control – Recurring processes that eliminate variances in the quality of Center services and support continuous improvement.
  • Quality Control Tracker – The HTML-based system used to document findings, track corrective actions, and generate reports; it links to L-Track and SL-Track.
  • L-Track and SL-Track – The primary loan trackers used by all staff for loan action flow and actions.
  • Genesys – The system used for verbal Customer Service interactions.
  • Customer Service Hub – The system used for written Customer Service interactions.
